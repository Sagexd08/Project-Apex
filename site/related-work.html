How to read this page

This page places Prism among prior work and narrows its claim to what survives the comparison. It follows the claim audit described in the project's research brief.

Honest limits

Each row summarizes what the cited source says about itself, as we understand it. We have not reproduced any of these results, and a full primary-source read of each paper is still on the roadmap. Treat every cell as Reported, not reproduced. If a row overlaps Prism, the Prism claim shrinks; it does not hide the overlap.

Already solved by prior work

  • Random rotation followed by scalar quantization is a known, data-oblivious compression recipe.
  • Binary and few-bit codes with error bounds exist for distance estimation.
  • Progressive refinement and reranking with full precision are standard in quantized search.
  • Immutable segments, snapshots and write-ahead logs are mature storage techniques.

Not solved by the designs we have reviewed

With the limit above (a surface read, not a full audit), these look open to us:

  • A bound on the pruning gate that is both provable and cheap enough to keep the saving.
  • One code layout that serves 1, 2, 3 and 4 bit scoring from a single stored copy, together with log-pinned readers.
  • Honest measurement of the same engine read-while-written, over hours, with tail latency reported next to absolute latency.

Potential Prism contribution, pending proof and measurement

The narrowest defensible statement today: nested bit planes from one stored code, combined with a log-pinned snapshot per reader, as a design to test for streaming search. Not the rotation, not scalar quantization, not progressive reranking, and not snapshots. Whether the combination helps is the experiment, and so far the only time comparison shows no saving.
