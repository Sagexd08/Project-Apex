Next, in order

  1. Close the gate problem. A per-vector error bound that is cheap enough to keep the saving, or a clear negative result.
  2. Real data. SIFT1M, GIST1M and a pinned text-embedding set, with checksums and their licences verified before use.
  3. One strong baseline. Reproduce a rotation-plus-quantization baseline on the same corpus and report both honestly.
  4. Snapshot model. A deterministic state-machine model with a crash at every publication boundary, and a linearizability-style history check.
  5. Long runs. At least an hour of concurrent reads and writes per major configuration on a quiet machine, with repeated trials.

What cannot be done from this machine

  • A macOS run and a CI matrix.
  • A true power-loss test.
  • A Linux peak-memory run and 10-million-vector or idle-machine reruns.
  • An independent review. Every result here is the author's, and none has been checked by someone else.
