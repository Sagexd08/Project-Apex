Algorithm tournament

Seven candidate designs, one common yardstick. No winner is declared until the evidence exists.

Unmeasured

Rules of the tournament

Seven candidates are compared on the same corpus, queries, metric, top-k, hardware and concurrency, with ground truth computed over the eligible set, not the whole corpus. Training time and the memory of the original vectors are counted for every method, not just some. No candidate is declared the winner because of its name.

State today

Only candidates A, C and part of D have any measurement, and only on one synthetic set. Every "keep", "defer" or "reject" below is a provisional plan, not a verdict. The full tournament has not been run.

What a fair comparison needs

  • Same machine, threads, normalization, metric convention, recall definition and preprocessing for every method.
  • Warm-up and cache state reported; enough queries for the p99 to mean something; repeated trials with an uncertainty estimate.
  • Training and build time for methods that need it. Original vectors included in every memory total.
  • A real dataset, a filtered dataset with real metadata, and adversarial synthetic sets. A synthetic run is never presented as a real-data result.

Selection gate

The tournament ends with one written decision per candidate: keep, defer or reject, with its evidence. Until then the honest summary is: one candidate has a first measurement, and its time comparison with the exact baseline is inconclusive (the sign reversed between two runs). Details of the open work are on the roadmap.
